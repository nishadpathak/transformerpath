Slot clues and your matches
  1. Between core and inner winding

    Look for the tube immediately outside the core.

  2. Between inner and outer windings

    Look for the dividing shell between the two windings.

  3. In a gap between winding discs

    Look for the small gap between stacked conductor discs.

  4. Along the vertical oil channel

    Look for the long strip running along, not across, the channel.

  5. Over the end of the winding stack

    Look above the top disc, at the axial end of the winding.

What this model shows

One illustrative core-type, liquid-filled arrangement. The diagram places the LV winding closer to the core and the HV winding outside it. An inter-disc spacer keeps adjacent discs apart and leaves paths for radial oil flow; a duct strip runs axially to hold open a channel. Real designs can use different winding and insulation arrangements.

Learning only. One slot stands for a component family; this is not an assembly order, dimensional design, or manufacturing instruction.

Component reference and sources
Inner pressboard cylinder
A cylindrical insulation support around the core-side of the inner winding.
Main insulation barrier
A separate cylindrical pressboard barrier in the insulation space between windings.
Radial inter-disc spacer
Separates adjacent winding discs at selected positions around the circumference, leaving radial oil-flow space between spacers.
Axial duct strip
Runs along the winding height to maintain separation and help leave space for axial oil flow.
Insulating end ring
A ring at the end of a winding stack; in this model it represents end insulation and support.
