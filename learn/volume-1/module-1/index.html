Module 1 — Specification to service

The path from an enquiry to a unit in service: which decision is made in the specification, which in the works, and which only after the first voltage.

Free module. This page is free to read. It does not ask you to sign in. It does not offer a class or a call. Use the edition of each standard named in the contract. Questions: hello@transformerpath.com.

Volume 1 is the practical path from specification to service. This module is the map of that path. It does not design a transformer, and it does not fill in a test sheet. It says, in order, where a decision has to be made if the unit that arrives is to be the unit that was bought.

The enquiry fixes more than a power and a voltage

A manufacturer cannot cut steel from a system voltage and a kilovolt-ampere figure alone. The enquiry has to name the base standard and the edition, then list the departures. Rated power belongs beside a cooling stage. Impedance is a value the enquiry states, because it sets fault current and voltage regulation. Voltages, the highest voltage for equipment, insulation levels, frequency, ambient, and altitude are all rating decisions. If losses are capitalised, the rates are part of the enquiry. If they are not, the guaranteed no-load loss and load loss still have to be asked for, because the factory test is judged against those guarantees.

A sentence that says the unit shall comply with IEC and IEEE names neither document. Temperature rise, test code, and tolerances then stay ambiguous. Two honest practices exist. IEC 60076-1 is the general requirement in IEC practice. IEEE C57.12.00 is the general requirement for many liquid-immersed units in IEEE practice. Pick the edition the contract will use. Do not convert one family’s numbers into the other from memory.

What is decided later, and cannot be assumed now

Design turns the enquiry into steel, copper or aluminium, insulation, and a tank. The works then does things that a drawing cannot undo: the core joint, the winding tension, the dry-out, the oil fill. Factory tests show whether this serial number meets the rating. Site tests show whether transport and assembly left that result intact. Only then does the unit take voltage. Each of those steps has its own module later in the course. Skipping one and hoping the next step will catch it is how a wrong vector group, a closed cooler valve, or a missing loss guarantee reaches service.

  • Name one base standard and its edition. List departures. Do not cite two families as if they were one.
  • Put a cooling stage next to every rated power. The higher number is not available with the fans stopped.
  • State impedance, frequency, and insulation levels. They are not implied by the voltage ratio.
  • Keep the factory result and the site result as two records. Transport sits between them.
