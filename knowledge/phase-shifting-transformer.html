What Is a Phase-Shifting Transformer (PST)?

Published 27 Aug 2026Updated 27 Aug 20267 min readAdvancedGrid
Quick answer — A phase-shifting transformer (PST), also called a quadrature booster, is a series unit that controls power flow by injecting a phase-shifted voltage. It changes the phase angle across the line, thereby redirecting real-power flow from overloaded lines to under-loaded parallel paths in a meshed network.

What is a phase-shifting transformer (PST) and what does it do?

What it controls

In an AC network, real power flows largely according to the phase-angle difference between two points. The PST adds a controllable phase shift, letting the operator adjust how much power flows on a given line.

Construction

A PST uses a series winding (in the line) and an excitation winding (fed from the bus), with an on-load tap-changer controlling the injected voltage and hence the phase angle.

Why it matters

PSTs relieve congestion, balance loaded and lightly loaded parallel lines, increase transfer capacity and prevent overloads — increasingly important in interconnected networks and markets.
