How Do You Compare Transformer Quotes?

Published 27 Aug 2026Updated 27 Aug 20267 min readIntermediateProcurement
Quick answer — Compare transformer quotes on the specification compliance first, then on total cost of ownership (losses, lead time, spares, warranty), not just the price. Normalise each bid to the same rated power, voltage, impedance, loss and test criteria, then assess delivery, reliability, documentation and after-sales. A lower price with higher losses or a longer lead time can cost more over the life.

How should you compare transformer quotes?

Check compliance first

Confirm each bid meets the same specification: MVA, voltage, impedance, vector group, losses, cooling, standards and tests. A quote that does not comply is not comparable.

Capitalise the losses

The no-load and load losses have a real energy cost over the life, often capitalised as a loss-evaluation factor. A unit with lower losses can justify a higher price.

Consider TCO and risk

Add lead time, warranty, spares, delivery, the manufacturer track record, and any installation/commissioning support. A longer lead time or a weak after-sales offer can be more costly than a small price saving.

Make a clean comparison table

Untracked fields — tappings, test levels, tank/fittings, noise, transport — can hide differences, so build a clear side-by-side before you decide.
