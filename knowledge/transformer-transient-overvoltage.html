What Is a Switching (Transient) Overvoltage on a Transformer?

Published 27 Aug 2026Updated 20 Oct 20266 min readAdvancedDesign
Quick answer — A switching overvoltage is a transient voltage spike caused by a switching operation (e.g. opening or closing a breaker, or re-energising a line, cable or reactor), which can stress the transformer insulation. It usually has a lower peak but a slower front and longer duration than a lightning surge (tens to thousands of microseconds), and can interact with the transformer, especially a lightly loaded or reactor-connected unit, leading to a flashover or insulation damage if the margin is not adequate.

What is a switching overvoltage and how does it affect a transformer?

What it is

A fast, oscillating voltage surge from a switching event, sometimes combined with the transformer resonance, giving a high overvoltage at a specific point.

The risk

It can stress the insulation at the line end or the winding, and with certain configurations (cable, reactor) can cause ferroresonance or a high transient, so the BIL and the margin matter.

Protection

Surge arresters, choosing the right switching sequence and the insulation margin, and a switching study protect against it and the overvoltage.
