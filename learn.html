Choosing core area and flux density, CRGO grades and stacking factor, and how silicon steel cuts hysteresis and eddy losses — setting iron weight and no-load loss. ⚡ Sizing Engine

First-pass volts-per-turn, net core cross-section, turns per phase, and conductor sizes from winding (phase) current. Defaults match Dyn11.

Validation example

Inputs. 3-phase, 1,000 kVA, 11 kV / 0.433 kV, Dyn11 (HV delta, LV star), 50 Hz, peak flux 1.65 T, current density 2.8 A/mm², K = 0.45.

Expected. Volts per turn 14.23 V, net core area 388 cm², HV turns 773 (delta, 11,000 V per winding), LV turns 17.6 → 18, HV line current 52.5 A, HV winding current 30.3 A, LV line and winding current 1,333 A, HV conductor 10.8 mm², LV conductor 476 mm². Rounding LV to 18 turns makes the real volts per turn 250.0 / 18 = 13.89 V. The flux density then falls to 1.65 × 13.89 / 14.23 ≈ 1.61 T, and HV becomes 11,000 / 13.89 ≈ 792 turns. A designer fixes the LV turns first and recomputes from there.

References. Line current is I = S / (√3 · U) for three phases, the rated-current relation in IEC 60076-1, with U in volts. Winding voltage is the line voltage divided by √3 for star and equal to the line voltage for delta. Winding current equals the line current for star and is the line current divided by √3 for delta. Turns are that phase voltage divided by volts per turn. Net core area uses the sinusoidal EMF equation E = 4.44 · f · N · B · A, rearranged as A = Eₜ / (4.44 · f · B). Conductor area is winding current divided by current density. K√kVA (total kVA) is an empirical starting value for volts per turn, not an IEC constant. K ≈ 0.45 is the value usually quoted for three-phase core-type oil-immersed power transformers (A. K. Sawhney, A Course in Electrical Machine Design, transformer design chapter).
