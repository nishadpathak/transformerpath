Winding ratio

a = VHV / VLV. Series and common turns are (VHV − VLV) : VLV, which is (a − 1) : 1. The series section is the voltage between the two line terminals, not the HV terminal voltage.

Throughput is the power through the terminals. The equivalent two-winding rating is S · (VHV − VLV) / VHV. Connection letters are printed as entered. They do not set a clock hour.

Impedance

Percent impedance at this throughput is uk% on both terminals. Ohms are Z = (uk/100) · U² / S, so the ohmic value scales with U². One uk% is not split into a common-winding percent. Impedance voltage is uk% × U. Symmetrical short-circuit current is I / (uk/100). Short-circuit power is S / (uk/100), in MVA.

Validation example. Inputs: throughput 100 MVA, HV 132 kV, LV 66 kV, uk 12.5%, connections D and Y. Winding ratio a = 2. Series:common turns 1 : 1. Series-section voltage 66 kV. LV line current 874.8 A, series current 437.4 A, common current 437.4 A. Equivalent rating 50 MVA. Percent impedance 12.5% on both terminals. Ohmic impedance 21.78 Ω on HV and 5.445 Ω on LV. Impedance voltage 16.5 kV on HV and 8.25 kV on LV. The plate prints D / Y and does not add a clock hour. References: IEEE C57.12.10 uses the co-ratio for the equivalent rating. IEC 60076-1 rated current is I = S / (√3 · U). IEC 60076-5 clause 4.1 is the symmetrical short-circuit current. Percent impedance is not divided by the square of the voltage ratio.

Method

Method (step-down autotransformer): series voltage = HV−LV, common = LV; series:common turns = (HV−LV):LV. Common-winding current Ic = ILV − IHV. Self (transformer) rating = S·(HV−LV)/HV — the autotransformer only processes this fraction of throughput, which is why it is smaller.

Short-circuit currents use the entered terminal uk%. Percent impedance at the same throughput is not divided by a². Ohmic impedance does scale with voltage squared. One uk% is not split into a common-winding percent; that split needs the coupled-winding equivalent circuit. IEC 60076-5 Table 1 is a minimum for two-winding units, not a 4 to 15 percent cap, and clause 4.1 is the symmetrical short-circuit current. Treat these figures as a check, not a manufacturer test certificate.
